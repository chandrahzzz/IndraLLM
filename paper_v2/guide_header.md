## 1. Conventions

* **Condition names** (small caps in the paper): EN-ORIG, EN-MATCH, CS-LAT, ROMAN, NATIVE, MIXED.
* **Model:** Qwen3.8-27B, API identifier `qwen/qwen3.8-27b`. The provider's underlying weights cannot be
  independently verified; the tokenizer is established.
* **Judges:**
  * J-QWEN: Qwen3.8-27B, temperature 0, evaluator system prompt; primary labels at 128 tokens.
  * J-OSS: gpt-oss-120b, a cross-family judge.
  * Stored: the original labels; EXP-002 was graded at temperature 0.3 and EXP-003 at temperature 0.
  * The two judges are always shown side by side. Neither is called more accurate, and no human labels
    exist.
* **"Accuracy"** means judge-assessed accuracy, never "factual recall".
* **Non-significant gaps** are reported as "not detected", with intervals. They are never described as
  equivalence, because no equivalence margin was pre-specified.
* **The 1,024-token run** was added after the 512-token results were seen, so it is exploratory.
* **Multiplicity:** follow-up p values are given with Holm adjustment within each budget–judge cell
  (Holm-3) and across all 18 follow-up tests (Holm-18).
* **p values:** two decimals if ≥ 0.1, three decimals between 0.001 and 0.1, "< 0.001" below that.

## 2. What the evidence supports (central claim)

*Evaluating multilingual LLMs across representation conditions is sensitive to output-token budgets and
evaluator choice; observed differences should not automatically be interpreted as representation
effects.*

| Finding | Supported wording | Do not say |
|---|---|---|
| MIXED − CS-LAT: −23 (128) → +4 (512) → −3 (1,024) under J-QWEN; −8, −6, −5 under J-OSS | The 128-token gap is highly sensitive to the output budget. At 512 and 1,024 tokens no gap is detected under either judge, and the intervals exclude the 128-token size | Mixed script harms recall; the conditions are equivalent |
| English − CS-LAT: −24 at 1,024 under J-QWEN (topic p 0.018; Holm-3 0.036; Holm-18 0.29); −7 under J-OSS (n.s.) | The English comparison depends on the judge and the budget; the topic-level result does not survive correction across the follow-up | English is superior across languages and settings |
| Judge agreement 74.7% → 80.0% → 81.3% (κ 0.47 → 0.61) | Agreement varies with the output set; disagreement is lopsided (J-QWEN more lenient) | Either judge is accurate |
| 1,024 tokens: 5 / 39 / 64 responses hit the cap (EN-MATCH / CS-LAT / MIXED) | The budget remains a possible confound for residual differences | 1,024 is an unconstrained condition |
| Fluent-speaker template review (all five languages) | Templates reviewed and judged natural and correct; a yes/no check of the templates, not of outputs | Prompts validated for full semantic equivalence; judges validated |

## 4. Claims you must not make

* That script alternation, mixed script or native script harms factual recall or reasoning.
* That code-switching universally reduces factual accuracy.
* Equivalence of conditions (no equivalence test was pre-specified).
* That any result is "robust" or survives "every level" without naming the budget, the judge and the
  correction used.
* That 1,024 tokens removes the budget confound.
* That either judge is more accurate, or any human–judge agreement figure.
* κ = 0.719, naturalness 4.74/5, or the 300-item "human validation" document (no raw annotation files
  exist).
* That the fluent-speaker review validated model outputs, semantic equivalence or the judges.
* That ALLaM-2-7B replicates anything.
* That tokenization is irrelevant.
* That the served weights equal the public checkpoint.
* Any causal mechanism.
* Generalisation beyond one model, 20 topics and five languages.
